Astrophysical Signal Processing & Fast Folding Algorithm (FFA) Principles

Theoretical Foundation

Radio Pulsar Detection in the Sub-Zero SNR Regime

Radio pulsars are rapidly rotating magnetized neutron stars whose lighthouse-like emission beams sweep across terrestrial dish antennas (such as GMRT, Parkes, and FAST). Because the emitted pulses traverse thousands of light years through the ionized Interstellar Medium (ISM) and arrive contaminated with cold-sky synchrotron noise and receiver front-end thermal noise, the digitized input signal typically arrives with a negative signal-to-noise ratio:

SNR_in = P_signal / P_noise < 0 dB (Typical: -15 dB to -3 dB) ⇒ Individual single pulses are completely invisible in the raw time-series.

Furthermore, frequency-dependent interstellar dispersion delays low radio frequencies relative to higher frequencies according to the Dispersion Measure (DM = ∫ n_e dl):

Δt_DM ≈ 4.1488 × 10³ × DM × ( ν_low² - ν_high² )‾¹ seconds

After incoherent filterbank dedispersion, the summed intensity baseband stream is digitized by an 8-bit flash ADC at 50 MSPS and fed into our FPGA hardware pipeline.

Why FFA Beats FFT for Pulsar Discovery

Standard Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) searches rely on summing high-order Fourier harmonics (r = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16...). However, for long-period pulsars (P > 100 ms) and narrow rotational duty cycles (δ = W/P ∼ 1% to 5%), pulse energy is dispersed across hundreds of weak harmonics, degrading FFT sensitivity by 30% to 60%.

The Fast Folding Algorithm (FFA, Staelin 1969) operates directly in the time domain, computing tree-structured vector additions across trial periods P_trial to form an exact matched-filter without harmonic sum degradation.

Coherent Folding Gain: The √N Law

By folding N_samples into M = 64 rotational phase bins at candidate period P0, uncorrelated zero-mean receiver Gaussian noise cancels out destructively, whereas coherent pulsar pulse power accumulates constructively:

SNR_folded = SNR_raw × √N_folds = SNR_raw × √( N_samples / P0 )

For our 1024-sample circular buffer and a trial period P0 = 80 clock cycles (1600 μs), the accelerator folds N_folds = 12.8 cycles, providing a +11.1 dB coherent processing gain that pulls weak signals comfortably above the 5.0σ astronomical discovery threshold!

UVM Constrained-Random Verification Architecture

Robustness Proven

To ensure the FPGA accelerator performs reliably under turbulent observational conditions rather than passing on artificial deterministic data, our UVM 1.2 testbench employs mathematical Monte Carlo constrained randomization in ffa_adc_item.sv and ffa_sequence.sv:

Stochastic Gaussian Noise Injection

  • Monte Carlo Noise Modeling: Pseudo-Gaussian white noise generated across 6 distinct SNR regimes: Clean (+20dB), High (+12dB), Medium (+6dB), Low (+0.5dB), Buried (<0dB), and Pure Noise.
  • False Alarm Rejection: Pure white noise without astrophysical pulses is fed to confirm the hardware peak detector does not trigger false positive UART frames.

Astrodynamic Parameter Space Randomization

  • Period Sweeps: Trial periods randomized across P ∈ [16, 256] clock cycles, covering millisecond pulsars up to slow pulsars.
  • Pulse Width & Duty Cycle: Widths randomized across W ∈ [2, 32] samples with strict astrophysical constraint W < P/4 (δ ∼ 2.5% to 25%).
  • Interstellar Dispersion Jitter: Microsecond phase jitter injected to stress timing robustness.
